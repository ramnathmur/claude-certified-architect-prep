### Corroboration — this is stronger than the CCAR-P case

Two things independently support the community domain list, and they should be weighed honestly
rather than dismissed as "community, therefore worthless."

**1. Six of the seven domain names are the official lesson titles, near-verbatim.**

| Community domain name | Official Academy lesson title | Match |
|---|---|---|
| Prompting and Task Execution | Prompting & Task Execution | verbatim |
| Output Evaluation and Validation | Evaluating & Validating Claude's Output | same content, reworded |
| Workflow Integration and Solution Design | Workflow Integration & Solution Design | verbatim |
| Configuration and Knowledge Management | Configuration & Knowledge Management | verbatim |
| Governance, Risk and Responsible Use | Governance, Risk & Responsible Use | verbatim |
| Troubleshooting and Optimisation | Troubleshooting & Optimization | verbatim |
| Product and Model Selection | Claude Platform & Model Foundations | partial — the one loose mapping |

**2. The claimed weightings track the official lesson minutes.** Taking the 381 content minutes
(389 total minus the 8-minute summary lesson) as the denominator:

| Domain | Lesson min | Share of content time | Claimed weight | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Output Evaluation | 74 | 19.4% | 21% | 1.6 |
| Workflow Integration | 63 | 16.5% | 16% | 0.5 |
| Platform & Model | 59 | 15.5% | 12% | 3.5 |
| Governance & Risk | 55 | 14.4% | 15% | 0.6 |
| Prompting & Execution | 53 | 13.9% | 14% | 0.1 |
| Configuration | 47 | 12.3% | 12% | 0.3 |
| Troubleshooting | 30 | 7.9% | 10% | 2.1 |

The rank order is identical for the top two and the bottom one, and five of seven sit within 1.6
points. Anthropic built the course against the blueprint, so instruction time tracking exam weight is
what you would expect if the community figures came from the real guide.

**What this changes, and what it does not.** It raises confidence enough to justify starting corpus
work on the six verbatim-matched domains now, because their *existence and subject matter* rest on an
Anthropic source. It does **not** authorise setting question quotas — a 3.5-point error on Domain 3
is two items on a 60-item paper, and the whole point of the discipline is that the guide, not an
inference, decides the count.

## Open questions for the official guide to answer

1. Exact question count — community says 60, uncorroborated by any Anthropic source.
2. Whether items are standalone or grouped into scenario blocks. CCAR-F drew 4 blocks of 15 from a
   pool of 6, and that structure changed how papers had to be generated. The community source for
   CCAO-F describes standalone items and does not mention blocks at all.
3. The real domain weightings, to the decimal.
4. Whether multiple-response items are scored all-or-nothing. They were on CCAR-F, where eight
   majority-right answers scored zero. This changes exam-day tactics more than any content fact.
5. The published objective list per domain. The CCAR-F score report exposed 37 objectives, which is
   the right granularity to build a corpus against.
6. Whether the pass mark, scoring scale, validity and closed-book policy stated program-wide in the
   FAQ apply to CCAO-F without variation.
