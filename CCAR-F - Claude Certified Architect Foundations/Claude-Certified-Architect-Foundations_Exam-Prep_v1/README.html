Read this first: what this pack is, and what it is not

This pack does not teach you the subject from zero. It assumes you have met the concepts somewhere, and it does four things to what you already know: compresses it, shows you how the exam asks about it, tests you under time, and tells you where you are weak.

If the concepts are new to you, start on the website below. Coming to Test-1.html cold, with no prior exposure, will produce a low score and very little learning.

Steps 1 to 3 overlap on purpose — they are passes over the same syllabus at increasing depth, not parts of one book. You do not need all three. With days rather than weeks, do step 1 and skip to step 4.

If you have very little time: steps 0, 1, 4 (with Hint on), 6, then 8. Hint mode reveals the answer and reasoning immediately after each question, which turns a test into a teaching session and is the fastest way to find out what you do not know.

The five domains — what D1 to D5 mean

Every file in this package labels content by domain code. The codes are the exam’s own five domains, and the percentage is that domain’s share of the scored paper. A card numbered D1-07 is the seventh card of Domain 1; a citation reading §1.7 points at section 1.7 of the Corpus.

CodeDomainWeightCoversLessons
D1Agentic Architecture & Orchestration27%Agent loops, coordinators, subagents, delegation, hooks7
D2Tool Design & MCP Integration18%Tool descriptions, error handling, MCP servers, built-in tools5
D3Claude Code Configuration & Workflows20%CLAUDE.md, skills, rules, permissions, sessions, CI6
D4Prompt Engineering & Structured Output20%Success criteria, schemas, batching, review loops6
D5Context Management & Reliability15%Memory, escalation, provenance, graceful degradation6

D1 is more than a quarter of the paper on its own; D5 is the smallest. When you are rationing study time, ration it by these weights.

Learning corpus/ — every file, exactly what it is

Exam-Day-Guide.html — 67 concept cards

Each card is in three parts: what it is, how the exam asks it, and traps — the distractors that will sit next to the right answer. The cards are filed under the exam’s 30 official task statements, so you can see coverage rather than guess at it. Ends with a one-page Trap index and the 12 official sample questions with the examiners’ reasoning. Tick “Got it” on a card once you can state the answer without reading; the counter filters to what is left.

The shortest complete pass over the syllabus. If you read one file here, read this one.

CCA-F_Concept-Atlas_v2.html — 124 cards, the widest coverage

One card per concept the exam tests, each carrying the fact, the situation the exam builds around it, the rule to remember, a short hook to carry in, and the one wrong option planted beside the right one. Organised as five domain pages plus a traps page and a coverage page.

Use it when the Guide felt too thin, or open the single domain you are least sure of.

CCA-F_Trap-Sheet_v1.html — 136 cards, from the question-setter’s side

The other files answer what is true. This one answers how a setter turns that into an item, and which wrong option is built to catch someone who half-knows it. Two lenses per card: Asked (the signal in the stem, the form of the ask, the answer direction) and Traps (the distractor, why it fails, its mould). Includes the ten distractor moulds, the six production scenarios every stem is built inside, a look-alikes page for concepts that are easy to confuse, and a page on where the exam’s framing differs from current product documentation.

Open it after your first practice test, once you have seen how the questions actually read.

CCA-F_Corpus_v1.html — 73 numbered source sections

The source layer the rest of the pack was written from, across five domain pages. Every answer explanation in the tests and every card on the Trap Sheet ends with a citation like §4.9, and every one of those is a live link that opens the exact section.

Not a document to read front to back. You arrive here from a question you got wrong.

CCA-F_One-Page-Sheet_v1.html — one printable side of A4

The five domains, 19 hard rules, 10 pairs that are easy to confuse, the escalation and enforcement rules, how to sit the paper, and the out-of-scope list. Click Print this sheet; the navigation drops away.

The last thing you read, not the first. It assumes you have already been through one of the references above.

Mock tests/ — every file, exactly what it is

Test-1.html … Test-7.html — seven full-length exams

60 single-choice questions across 4 scenario blocks, weighted to the official domain breakdown, a 120-minute countdown, pass line at 720. Independent of each other — taking one does not affect any other. Progress saves as you go.

Test-MR.html — the multiple-response drill

30 questions that each state how many options to select and score all-or-nothing. This is the item type Tests 1–7 do not contain, and it is where a partly-right answer scores exactly the same as a blank one.

Dashboard.html — the results tracker

Score trend across attempts, average performance by domain, which domain takes you longest per question, a table of every attempt, and Study this links from each domain straight to the pages that cover it. Read the “What your score actually means” card before drawing a conclusion from any single number.

How the practice tests work

Open any test in a browser. Progress saves automatically, so you can close the tab and come back.

The Hint toggle sits in the top bar next to the timer and is off by default.

  • Hint off — behaves like the real exam. No feedback until you finish. Just the countdown.
  • Hint on — each question reveals the correct answer and the reasoning as soon as you answer it, and a running right/wrong count replaces the timer.

You can flip it at any point, including mid-test. Turning it on reveals every question you have already answered. An answer is locked once chosen, whichever way the switch is set.

Use Hint on for learning and Hint off for measuring. Do at least one full test with Hint off before exam day.

Every explanation ends with a Source line naming a corpus section — those are links into the Corpus.

To reset a test: open Jump to question, click Show results now, then Reset this exam. The MR drill has its own Reset button.

How the dashboard works

Finish a test, click “Copy results JSON” on its results page, open Dashboard.html, paste into the box, click “Add result.” Pasting the same test again replaces its earlier entry. The MR drill exports in the same format and slots in alongside the seven full tests.

Everything is computed in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.

The dashboard is where you decide what to study next. A weak domain needs reading — the Study this links open that domain in the Guide, the Atlas, the Trap Sheet and the Corpus. A slow domain needs practice under the clock.

What is not on the exam

The official guide puts these out of scope. If an answer option leans on one of them, that is a signal against it — and none of it needs revising.

  • Fine-tuning or training custom models
  • API authentication, billing, account management
  • Language and framework implementation detail beyond tool and schema configuration
  • Deploying or hosting MCP servers (infrastructure, networking, containers)
  • Claude’s internal architecture, training, weights
  • Constitutional AI, RLHF, safety training
  • Embedding models, vector databases
  • Computer use (browser and desktop automation)
  • Vision and image analysis
  • Streaming API and server-sent events
  • Rate limits, quotas, pricing calculations
  • OAuth, key rotation, auth protocols
  • Cloud-provider-specific configuration
  • Benchmarking or model comparison
  • Prompt caching detail beyond knowing it exists
  • Token counting and tokenization specifics

Two things to know before you rely on this

Tests 1–7 are single-choice; the real exam is not entirely. Those seven give you one correct answer of four throughout. The real paper also contains multiple-response items that state how many options to select and score all-or-nothing. Test-MR.html covers that item type; sit it at least once.
