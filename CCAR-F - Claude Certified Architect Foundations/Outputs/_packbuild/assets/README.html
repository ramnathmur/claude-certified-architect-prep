01What this is

A complete self-study pack for the Claude Certified Architect — Foundations exam, built and used by a colleague preparing for the certification, and shared so you do not have to build it yourself.

The real exam is 60 questions in 120 minutes, delivered as four scenario-based blocks drawn from a bank of six. It is scored on a scale of 100–1000, and 720 is the pass mark. Questions test practical architectural judgement — which fix actually addresses a root cause, which mechanism gives a guarantee rather than a probability — rather than recall of API signatures.

The five knowledge domains and their weightings on the real exam:

DomainWeightWhat it covers
D1 Agentic Architecture & Orchestration27%The agentic loop, coordinator and subagent design, delegation, error propagation, escalation
D2 Tool Design & MCP Integration18%Tool descriptions, structured errors, MCP servers and resources, hooks, built-in tools
D3 Claude Code Config & Workflows20%CLAUDE.md, rules, skills, slash commands, plan mode, CI/CD integration
D4 Prompt Engineering & Structured Output20%Few-shot, schemas, tool_use for structure, validation, retries, batch processing
D5 Context Management & Reliability15%Statelessness, lost-in-the-middle, context strategies, confidence calibration, provenance

Every paper in this pack matches those proportions, so a weak domain shows up in your results the same way it would on the real thing.

02Start here

If you have five minutes, do this:

  1. Open the Refresher and skim it. It is the fastest orientation to what the exam actually asks.
  2. Open Paper 1 and answer ten questions. Read the explanation after each one, including the ones you get right.
  3. That is the whole loop. Everything else in this pack supports it.

If you have an exam date booked, jump to the study plans and work backwards from it.

Everything runs locally

Every file here is a self-contained HTML page. Double-click it and it opens in your browser. Your answers and progress are saved in that browser's local storage — nothing is uploaded, and nobody else can see your results.

If answers do not save between sessions, your browser is probably restricting storage for files opened directly from disk. Run open-locally.bat (Windows, needs Python) to serve the folder at http://localhost:8000 instead, which resolves it.

03How to take a practice paper

This is the part worth reading properly. These papers are built as learning tools, not silent simulators, and using them like a mock exam wastes most of their value.

The single most important habit

When you answer a question, the paper immediately shows you why the correct answer is correct and why each of the other three is wrong, with a citation. Read all of it — especially on questions you answered correctly.

The wrong options are not filler. Each one encodes a specific, documented misconception: the fix that treats a symptom, the over-engineered solution, the prompt-level answer where only a code-level guarantee will do. Those same misconceptions are what the real exam's distractors are built from. If you only read the explanation when you get something wrong, you are learning from a quarter of the material.

Answers lock when you pick them

This is deliberate. You cannot change an answer once selected, which stops the drift toward second-guessing that makes practice scores meaningless. Commit, then read.

Pace yourself against the real budget

The real exam gives you 120 minutes for 60 questions — two minutes each. The papers show a running timer but never impose a countdown or cut you off. For your first two or three papers, ignore the clock entirely and read every explanation properly; you are learning, not measuring. Once you are scoring consistently, take one paper watching the clock to confirm pace is not a problem. Most people find it is not: the questions are long to read but quick to decide once the underlying principle is familiar.

Multiple-response questions — Papers 11 and 12

The official exam guide states the exam contains "multiple-choice and multiple-response items; each item states how many responses to select." Papers 11 and 12 each carry thirteen of them: nine asking for two of five options, four asking for three of six.

They show a Select N banner and a live count, and they are scored all or nothing — every correct option and no incorrect one. Read the count before you read the options. The most common way to lose these is identifying the right idea and then picking the wrong number of items.

Reading the results screen

When you finish, you get a raw score out of 60, an estimated scaled score against the 720 pass line, and a breakdown two ways: by domain against the official weights, and by scenario block. Papers 11 and 12 additionally split single-answer from multiple-response accuracy.

The two breakdowns answer different questions. A weak domain means a knowledge gap you can go and close in the reference sheets. A weak block with healthy domains usually means the scenario context threw you rather than the subject matter — which is worth knowing, because the real exam draws its scenarios at random.

Navigation while you work

  • Jump to question in the top bar opens a map of all 60, colour-coded by whether you have answered them. You can jump anywhere.
  • You can stop at any point and close the tab. Reopening the paper puts you back at the first unanswered question.
  • Show results now ends the paper early and scores what you have answered.
  • Print full exam renders every question and explanation as one document, if you would rather read on paper.

Do not sit two papers in one day

Each paper takes 45–90 minutes if you read the explanations properly, and the value comes from the reading rather than the answering. Two in a day means skimming the second. One paper plus a drill session beats two papers, every time.

04The four scenario blocks

The real exam frames its questions inside four production scenarios, drawn at random from a bank of six:

  • Customer Support Resolution Agent — an agent handling returns, billing disputes and account issues through MCP tools, judged on first-contact resolution
  • Code Generation with Claude Code — using Claude Code across a codebase, with CLAUDE.md configuration, custom commands and plan mode
  • Multi-Agent Research System — a coordinator delegating to specialised subagents that search, analyse, synthesise and report
  • Developer Productivity with Claude — tooling that explores unfamiliar codebases and automates repetitive work using built-in tools and MCP
  • Claude Code for Continuous Integration — automated review and test generation inside a CI/CD pipeline
  • Structured Data Extraction — extracting from unstructured documents, validating against JSON schemas, handling edge cases

Each paper's landing card tells you which four it drew. Those four were chosen deliberately rather than randomly, so that across the twelve papers you meet all six scenarios — which the real exam does not guarantee you in any single sitting. If you only have time for two papers, Papers 11 and 12 between them cover all six.

06Keeping score — no tools or accounts needed

Everything you need to track progress is already in the pack. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to install.

After each paper, its results screen gives you a Copy results JSON button. That JSON has two uses, and the whole ritual takes about a minute:

After every paper — the one-minute ritual

  1. On the results screen, click Copy results JSON.
  2. Open the drill deck → Import view → paste → import. The deck finds the cards matching what you got wrong and moves them to the front of your schedule. Your next drill session is automatically weighted to your gaps.
  3. Open the progress tracker → paste → Add result. It records the attempt and shows your score trend and per-domain accuracy across every paper you have logged.

You do not need the JSON to see how you did. The results screen already gives you the full picture — raw score, scaled estimate against the pass line, per-domain and per-block breakdowns. The JSON exists so the drill deck and the tracker can act on your results automatically, rather than you re-typing them.

The tracker's per-domain view is the one to watch over time. It aggregates across every paper you log, so after three or four papers it tells you with reasonable confidence which domain is genuinely weakest — and each domain's exam weighting is shown alongside, so you can see where revision buys the most marks. A 60% in D1 costs you far more than a 60% in D5.

07Two study plans

Pick whichever matches the time you actually have. Both assume roughly an hour a day.

The two-week plan

Comfortable · about 14 hours total
Days 1–2Read the Refresher, then the Masterclass. Take Paper 1 untimed, reading every explanation.
Days 3–6One paper a day (Papers 2–5). After each: import into the drill deck, log in the tracker. Start each session with ten minutes of drill.
Day 7No paper. Read the deep reference sheet for whichever domain the tracker says is weakest. Drill only.
Days 8–11Papers 6–9, same routine. You should see the scaled score stabilising.
Day 12Paper 11 — your first with multiple-response questions. Take it timed.
Day 13Paper 12, timed. Between 11 and 12 you have now seen all six scenarios.
Day 14No paper. The cram sheet, plus a full drill pass over everything still in your Missed pile.

The five-day plan

Compressed · when the exam is close
Day 1Refresher, then Paper 11 untimed, reading everything. It is the broadest single paper here.
Day 2Import and log Day 1's result. Drill. Read the deep reference for your weakest domain.
Day 3Paper 12, timed. Import, log, drill.
Day 4Any earlier paper, timed. Import, log. Long drill session.
Day 5No paper. Cram sheet plus drill. Rest properly the night before.

A scaled score consistently above 800 across two or three papers is a reasonable signal of readiness. Below 720, the tracker's per-domain view will tell you exactly where to spend the next session.

08Notes and limitations

This material is unofficial. It was built independently from Anthropic's published exam guide and documentation. It is not endorsed by Anthropic, and it contains no real exam questions — every question here is original, written to match the published domain weightings, scenario bank and question style.

Get the official guide. Anthropic publishes the authoritative exam guide — domain weightings, task statements, the scenario bank and sample questions — on its certification pages. Read it. It is the one source that is definitive, and it is short. This pack deliberately does not bundle a copy so that you get the current version first-hand rather than a snapshot.

The scaled score is an approximation. It is computed as round((correct / 60) × 900 + 100). The real exam uses psychometric scaling across equated forms, so treat the number as a rough gauge of readiness, not a prediction of your result.

Paper 10 has three known cosmetic defects — one question closely resembling another paper's, one over-long answer option, and a repeated closing phrase within a block. None affects whether its answers are correct. It is included because the content is sound.

Your data stays with you. Every page stores progress in your own browser's local storage. Nothing is transmitted anywhere. Clearing your browser data or switching browsers will reset your progress, so use the tracker's Copy all my results button if you want a backup.
