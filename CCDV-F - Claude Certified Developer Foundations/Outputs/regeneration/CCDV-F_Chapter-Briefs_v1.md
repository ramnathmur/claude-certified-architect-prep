## 1. The three ledgers

These are the anti-template mechanism. They are assigned here, once, across all 34 chapters, because a
collision is only visible globally — you cannot pick a form for chapter 20 without knowing what 19 and
21 used.

### Rules

1. **Form** — no form in two consecutive chapters; no form more than three times in 34. Twelve forms ×
   three = 36 slots against 34 chapters, so every reuse must be earned rather than defaulted to.
2. **Anchor** — 34 real-world anchors, each used once and retired. A repeat is a defect caught in
   review. This is the rule that prevents the everything-is-a-restaurant-kitchen collapse.
3. **Opening** — six opening moves, no two consecutive chapters sharing one.

### Verification

Form counts: 1×3 · 2×3 · 3×3 · 4×3 · 5×3 · 6×2 · 7×3 · 8×3 · 9×2 · 10×3 · 11×3 · 12×3 = **34.**
No form appears in adjacent chapters — checked pairwise across all 33 adjacencies.
No opening appears in adjacent chapters — checked pairwise across all 33 adjacencies.
34 anchors, 34 distinct.

## 2. What every brief assumes

**Internal shape**, from design v1 §4, in this order: the situation (~150 w) · the mechanism (~40%) ·
the derivation (~15%) · the break (~15%) · the boundary (~10%) · the tell (~50 w) · self-test.

**The derivation chain**, from design v1 §5: teach the causal mechanism one level below the rule →
derive the rule out loud → break it, showing a surface match lose to the mechanism → name the tell.

**Self-test:** 3–5 items in the exam's shape — short scenario, four options, one plausible-but-irrelevant
lever, one non-enforceable control, one bigger-hammer. MC or MR, count stated. Never fill-in-the-blank.
**Chapters 32, 33 and 34 carry 8–10 items instead of 3–5** — see design v2 §4.

**The admission test on every fact:** could this be the difference between two of four options in a
scenario item? If not, it goes to the marked reference appendix, and no self-test item may test
anything there.

**Does not earn a place:** learning-objective lists · summary boxes · definitions of terms the chapter
never uses · restating the mechanism in other words for emphasis · "why this matters" preambles.

### Part I — The substrate

**Ch 1 · The one budget everything spends** · 2,400 w · LLM Fundamentals 1,600 · Context Engineering 800
**Idea:** everything the model does is paid for out of one finite pot, and the pot is re-filled and
re-charged on every single request.
**Owns:** `Tokens` · `context windows`
**Form 3 budget ledger · Open D a number · Anchor:** loading a shipping container to a fixed volume.
**Source:** corpus (M1, M2).
**Boundary:** owns the fixed-pot concept only. **Context-window *management* is ch.8's** — this chapter
does not teach pruning, compaction or clearing. It must make the reader feel the ceiling, not manage it.
**Must land:** the window holds system prompt, history, tool definitions, tool results and output at
once; the two edge behaviours — rejected before generation versus truncated mid-generation — are
different failures with different fixes.

**Ch 2 · Why the same prompt answers twice differently** · 1,600 w · LLM Fundamentals 1,600
**Idea:** the model draws one token at a time from a distribution, so variation is the mechanism working
as designed rather than the system failing.
**Owns:** `sampling` · `non-determinism` · `next-token generation`
**Form 9 physical analogue · Open A a symptom · Anchor:** an ensemble weather forecast.
**Source:** corpus (M1, M2).
**Boundary:** does not teach temperature as a tuning recipe, and does not teach evals — ch.26 owns what
you do about non-determinism in testing. This chapter ends at *why assertions must be on properties*.
**Must land:** what the distribution is over, since the contract flags `next-token generation` as
implied-only if the chapter says only "sampling from a distribution".

**Ch 3 · Two dials, not one** · 2,600 w · Model Selection 1,800 · LLM Fundamentals 800
**Idea:** which model, and how hard it thinks, are two separate decisions — and they are priced,
tuned and broken independently.
**Owns:** `thinking` · `fast mode` · `extended thinking` · `adaptive thinking` · `effort levels` ·
`Opus vs Sonnet vs Haiku use cases` · `adaptive thinking support` · `quality/latency/cost tradeoffs` ·
`breaking behaviour changes across model releases`
**Form 2 two-column fork · Open F a term he owns ("thinking") · Anchor:** a translation bureau — who
translates, and how long they are given, are two separate purchase decisions.
**Source:** **Pack A.** Every claim cited.
**Boundary:** cost arithmetic is ch.9's. This chapter decides *which* model and *how much thinking*;
it does not compute a bill.
**Must land:** **extended thinking and adaptive thinking are named separately in the blueprint and must
be taught as two things**, with which models support adaptive. Name the tiers — the contract flagged
`Opus vs Sonnet vs Haiku use cases` as implied-only because the old line said "model tier" and never
named a tier. Nine sub-topics in 2,600 words is the densest ratio in Part I: this chapter earns its
length by being a fork, not a list.

**Ch 4 · What is actually on the wire** · 3,100 w · Technical Fundamentals 2,400 · Claude API Mechanics 700
**Idea:** an SDK is a typed wrapper over an HTTP request, and everything it hides is still happening.
**Owns:** `REST APIs` · `JSON` · `invoking Claude through third-party vendors` ·
`Foundational technical concepts for AI application development` · `integrating with SDKs that wrap REST APIs`
**Form 12 end to end · Open C a physical process · Anchor:** one letter, posted through different
carriers — same letter, different rules, different guarantees.
**Source:** corpus for REST/JSON/SDK; **Pack A adjacent** for vendors. Cite the vendor claims.
**Boundary:** **names the Agent SDK only to contrast two SDK layers — ch.16 owns it.** Does not teach
request shapes; that is ch.5.
**Must land:** the third-party vendor decision (first-party API against Bedrock against Vertex) and what
it decides about region, auth and compliance. This was absent from the design entirely until Stage 1.
**Open risk:** `Foundational technical concepts…` is unenumerable in the guide. This chapter needs a
**breadth** strategy, not depth — see design v2 §6 note 1. Flag to Ram if it cannot be discharged.

**Ch 5 · Who is waiting?** · 2,600 w · Technical Fundamentals 1,800 · SWE Foundations 800
**Idea:** latency tolerance is the axis — if nobody is waiting for the answer, speed is something you
are paying for and not using.
**Owns:** `asynchronous programming` · `Messages API data access patterns` · `batch API` ·
`realtime-vs-batch tradeoffs` · `websockets`
**Form 11 inventory audit · Open E a constraint from outside engineering · Anchor:** a restaurant's
service modes — à la carte, counter, catering order, delivery.
**Source:** corpus (M2, M4).
**Boundary:** **names streaming only inside the choose-a-shape decision — ch.12 owns streaming.**
**Must land:** websockets are named in the blueprint and appear nowhere in the corpus. Treat them
honestly: say what they are, and say why Claude's streaming uses SSE instead. An honest short treatment
beats a confident wrong one.

**Ch 7 · When asking nicely stops working** · 2,200 w · Output Handling 2,200

**Owns:** `Structured output patterns` · `response validation` · `defensive parsing` ·
`skepticism toward confident output`

**Source:** **Pack A.** Every claim cited.

**Ch 8 · Keeping a long session inside the budget** · 2,400 w · Context Engineering 2,400

**Ch 9 · Paying once for what does not change** · 2,200 w · Cost and Token Management 2,200

## 4. Standing warnings for every author agent

1. **The gate is not advisory.** `CCDV-F_Prose-Gate_v1.md` C1–C14, run blind by a different agent.
   Two rounds maximum, then it escalates to Ram. A FAIL on C3 (negation tricolon), C6 (reader
   mind-reading) or C11 (banned constructions) means the chapter is rewritten from outline, not patched.
2. **C14 — analogy fidelity — is new and catches the specimen's own defect.** Build a table of every
   analogue against the referent it maps onto, and check no sentence gives the referent a property
   contradicting the analogue. The gated specimen fails this: it names *"who owns the capability when it
   changes"* as the discriminator, then maps an externally-owned subscription onto an MCP server the
   reader builds and owns. **34 chapters carry 34 anchors, so this is a systemic risk, not a one-off.**
3. **A checkable fact gets one flat sentence.** No setup, no invented false belief to negate, no
   narrative wrapper. This is a standing hard ban in Ram's global instructions, and it is what the
   previous attempt was rejected for.
4. **Never tell the reader what they were thinking.** Zero tolerance, and it is the check the whole
   regeneration exists to satisfy.
5. **Volatile facts go to the marked appendix.** The guide is v1.0, July 2026, and states it is subject
   to change without notice. Anything version-specific is quarantined so the quarterly re-check has a
   bounded surface instead of 34 chapters.
6. **DV-01.** Build the file as a skeleton first, then fill section by section with separate edits of
   roughly 300 lines. Report which sections you filled. A single large write killed a generating agent
   on this project once already.
7. **State plainly what you verified by checking against a source versus what you only reasoned about.**
