A hardship-offer assistant, one European bank

It reads account history and correspondence, proposes a restructuring offer, and drafts the letter. EU personal data is in scope, the offers are consequential decisions, and the correspondence it reads is untrusted third-party text. Five objectives, one question underneath all of them: does this control hold regardless of what the model generates?

5.1Remove the capability, not the intent

The assistant holds no tool that can amend an account. It emits a structured offer object, and a separate service validates it against term limits and eligibility, holding the only credential that matters. The failure class "the model amended an account it should not have" does not exist, rather than being mitigated by an instruction not to.

A guardrail holds independently of what the model generates; four layers beat one because their failure modes are uncorrelated. Scoped permissions is the strongest layer, because it is not a check at a moment, so no moment can be too late for it.

The reflexDoes this control hold regardless of what the model generates? A strengthened prompt going from 97% to 99.4% compliance is still a tendency, and at volume it is still hundreds of daily failures.

5.2Wrap retrieved content as untrusted data

Correspondence is wrapped as untrusted data, so the eligibility decision never depends on instructions hidden inside it. Confabulated policy terms are caught by validating every cited term against the actual policy database. Injection is neutralized by the plain absence of privilege, and version drift is handled by pinning the model and running a 200-case regression set before any change.

Two facts generate the whole catalogue: the model samples plausible continuations, and instructions and data arrive through the same channel. A stated confidence is text from the same process as the claim, not a measurement of it.

The tellConfidently wrong right after a document refresh points at retrieval and indexing, not the model. The timing is the evidence, and a bigger model just phrases the same wrong passage more fluently.

5.3Route on consequence and independent confidence

Every declined offer, and every offer above a defined concession value, goes to a case handler regardless of confidence, because those are the outcomes a customer feels and a regulator asks about. Offers inside standard bands where the model and validator agree auto-issue, with 2% audited weekly. The reviewer's screen shows arrears history, the specific policy clause, and the proposed terms side by side.

Consequence and confidence cross into a routing table, but past a consequence threshold confidence stops being a routing variable at all. Confidence has to come from something independent of the model: a verifier, a score, a rule, never the model's own stated certainty.

Wrong axisRouting on the model's self-reported confidence is the domain's purest distractor, phrased to sound identical to the right answer. The difference is where the number came from.

5.4Enforce the boundary before assembly, not after

The payload is arrears history, account terms and stated circumstances, not the full customer record. Name, account number and national ID are tokenized before the call and re-associated locally. Processing runs against an EU-region endpoint. Logs from this purpose are walled off from the marketing pipeline as an access control, not as a policy statement.

A regulation is an obligation that must hold regardless of system behaviour, so it can only be enforced architecturally. What crosses the boundary is decided field by field before assembly, never by asking the model afterward.

The attractive wrong answerAn instruction not to repeat an identifier is evaluated after the identifier is already in the request. The transmission the regulation cares about has already happened.

5.5Measure by subgroup, on a written definition

Decline-rate parity is measured monthly across the bank's accountable subgroups on a stratified set, chosen because wrongful decline is the harm being controlled. Every decision carries a full trace. Removing a protected attribute would not remove the signal, since postal code, school name and phrasing register still carry it, and it would delete the very column needed to measure the disparity.

Fairness definitions genuinely conflict when base rates differ, so the design has to declare which one it holds itself to, in writing. Explainability is a reconstructable trace; a generated rationale is plausible text, not a record of what produced the decision.

Two failures, same shapeRemoving the protected attribute and adding a prompt-level fairness statement both feel principled. One deletes the measurement; the other adds a tendency where the requirement was a measurement.

One question closes the domain. Does this control hold regardless of what the model generates? A prompt instruction, a documented risk, a bigger model, a routing rule on self-reported confidence, and an aggregate accuracy figure are five different-looking answers that all fail it the same way: each is a tendency standing in for a guarantee.
