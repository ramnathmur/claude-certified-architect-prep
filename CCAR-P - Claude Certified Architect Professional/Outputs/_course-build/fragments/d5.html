Domain 5 carries 14% of the exam, about 9 of the 63 items, across five objectives. It is about what a system must refuse, what it must be able to prove, and who it must answer to.

Items here tend to offer a control that sounds responsible and acts at the wrong moment or on the wrong thing.

Examples are a log written after the data left, a prompt instruction against an absolute rule, a model checking its own work, and a confidence figure the model reports about itself. The test is whether the control is deterministic, independent of the model and early enough. The table below is the course's reading of the domain, not the guide's wording.

ObjectiveThe question it answers
Guardrails and safety controlsWhich independent layers stand between a bad input and a consequential action?
Risks, limitations and failure modesWhat is the symptom's signature, and which control is independent of the model?
Human-in-the-loop validationWhich items need a person, and how is the rest checked?
Regulatory complianceDoes the control act before the data crosses, and which constraint binds?
Ethical AIWhich group is the aggregate hiding, and what record explains a decision?

The guide's three samples sit in Domains 2, 3 and 4, so Domain 5 has none of its own. Two of them bear on it. The least-privilege item, Sample 1, is the logic of the least-privilege section of the guardrails chapter. The RAG-after-refresh item, Sample 3, is the first rule in the risks chapter's failure-mode list.
