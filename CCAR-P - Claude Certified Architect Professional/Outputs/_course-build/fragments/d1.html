Seventeen percent of the exam is Domain 1, about 11 of the 63 items. Its six objectives form one chain, and each step takes the output of the one before.

  1. TranslateA business states an outcome. Find the decision inside the workflow that a model can own.
  2. DesignWire that decision into input, processing, output and feedback.
  3. SelectPick the simplest pattern that meets the stated requirements.
  4. OrchestrateAdd agents only where one agent cannot do the job.
  5. DecomposeSplit whatever is still too big to evaluate as one unit.
  6. AlignExpress the whole design in the unit the business measures.

Every step constrains the next. A wrong answer in this domain is usually a design that works in isolation and has no justification in the requirement the scenario states.

One idea sits at the centre. The patterns form a ladder: a plain model call, an augmented LLM (the model plus retrieval, tools or memory), a workflow (fixed steps in your code), an agent (the model decides the path) and multi-agent (a coordinator plus specialists). Move right only when the rung to your left demonstrably cannot meet a stated requirement. Each step right buys adaptivity and costs predictability, tokens, latency variance and debugging effort.

Foundations tested whether a design is correct. Professional tests whether it survives production and whether you can defend it to the person holding the budget. Carry four questions into every scenario: what does it cost at volume, what breaks first under real traffic, how would you explain it to a non-engineer, and what does a regulated sector add.
