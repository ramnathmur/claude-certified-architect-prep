Domain 4 carries 16% of the exam, about 10 of the 63 items, across six objectives. The earlier domains build the system. This one asks whether the system works, how you would know, and what to change when it does not.

Items usually hand you a number or a symptom: a 96% score, a 97% aggregate, a bill that tripled, confident answers that are wrong. The question is what that evidence permits. A score on a biased set, an aggregate over a failing segment and a mean over a bad tail each look like evidence and carry less than they seem to. The table below is the course's reading of the domain, not the guide's wording.

ObjectiveThe question it answers
Define evaluation metricsWhat counts as a pass, and who set the bar before the results?
Datasets and test frameworksWhat is the set made of, who grades it, and how are attempts counted?
A/B testing and iterationHow does a change reach users without hiding its effect?
Diagnose system issuesWhich layer does the symptom point at?
Optimise tokens, latency and costWhich lever saves, and loses nothing the requirement needs?
Monitor with loggingWhat can a production log attribute to a layer and a release?

The one official Domain 4 sample gives a symptom with a timing clue. A RAG system returns confident but wrong answers after a document refresh, while latency and model version are unchanged. The right answer investigates retrieval and indexing first. It is worked through in the diagnosis chapter of this domain.
