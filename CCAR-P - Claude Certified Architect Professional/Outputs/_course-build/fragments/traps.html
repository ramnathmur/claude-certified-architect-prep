These labels are this project's. They come from tagging the wrong options in the practice corpus and in the guide's three samples. The guide gives a rationale for each of its three samples but no taxonomy of wrong answers, so treat the names as a study aid and the questions as the useful part. A wrong option can fit more than one family.

HALF-MOVE

Correct as far as it goes, but it leaves a stated requirement untouched. Showing the heading path to the model after retrieval helps it read what it got and does nothing for which chunks come back (§3.9).

Ask: does this option still leave any requirement in the stem unmet?

WRONG-AXIS

A real technique applied to the wrong dimension of the problem. Doubling chunk size when the chunks lack context, or lowering the temperature when the fault is in retrieval (§3.9, §3.10).

Ask: what is the stem actually about, and does this option act on that?

REPAIR

Rebuilds downstream what an earlier step should have done cleanly. A firmer system prompt for a sequence that needs a programmatic precondition is the standard case (§1.10).

Ask: which earlier stage could have prevented this, and does the option use it?

DISCARD

Solves the problem by replacing or throwing away something the scenario still needs. A more capable model when the missing piece is the feedback path (§1.9), or truncating a policy the answer depends on (guide Sample 2).

Ask: what working part does this option throw away?

ARCHITECTED

Sounds like the mature, professional design, more thorough or more unified than the stem asks for, but does not solve the problem the stem states. A monitoring dashboard as the improvement mechanism is the standard case (§1.9), and so is one unified index that embeds a table meant for filtering (§3.8).

Ask: does this solve the stated problem, or does it only sound more complete?

OVERSPEC

The strength of the guarantee does not match the requirement. It is either stronger than asked, such as a blocking control for a mere preference, or only statistical where the requirement is absolute, such as a 99.4% compliance rate standing in for a rule that must never break (§5.3).

Ask: is the requirement "always" or "prefer", and does the mechanism match?

EVIDENCE-MISMATCH

Offers a cause that the evidence in the stem does not support or already rules out. Sample 3's "the model weights silently changed" when the stem says the model version is unchanged, and its "the temperature is too low", which nothing about a document refresh would trigger.

Ask: which facts does the stem state, and which options contradict them?

DETECTIVE-FOR-PREVENTIVE

Detects or confirms a misuse where the requirement is to remove the capability. Sample 1's audit logging and confirmation prompt, when the role never needs the refund or delete tools.

Ask: does the stem say the capability is unneeded? Then removal is the answer.
