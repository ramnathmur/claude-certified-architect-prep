Domain 6 carries 14% of the exam, about 9 of the 63 items, across five objectives. It follows an engagement from the first meeting to a system someone else runs: what to ask for, how to report, what to promise, what to write down, and what to hand over.

Most items here describe a conversation or a document and offer an answer that is accurate, polite and too early or too general. Build the named pattern, quote the aggregate, promise the pilot number, hand over the repository. The test is usually whether the answer gives the stakeholder something they can decide with, or hold you to, or check. The table below is the course's reading of the domain, not the guide's wording.

ObjectiveThe question it answers
Discovery and requirement gatheringWhat decision does the system serve, and how will anyone verify it?
Communicating decisions and trade-offsWhat number does the sponsor need, and what must be named as a limit?
Feedback loops and expectationsWhich metric fits this person's decision, and what can be promised?
Documentation and implementation guidanceCan a reader who was not there tell why, and change it safely?
Lifecycle phasesWhat breaks between pilot and scale, and who can tell when it stops working?

The guide's three samples sit in Domains 2, 3 and 4, so Domain 6 has none of its own.
