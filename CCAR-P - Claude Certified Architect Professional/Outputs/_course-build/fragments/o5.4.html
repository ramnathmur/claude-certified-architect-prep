A healthcare client requires that patient identifiers never leave its network. The team adds a line to the system prompt: "do not repeat or store patient identifiers". Evaluation shows the assistant obeys it 99.4% of the time. At 50,000 requests a day, that leaves 300 replies a day that repeat an identifier. The figures are illustrative.

The larger problem comes before the reply. All 50,000 requests have already carried the identifiers to the inference endpoint, and the instruction is read after they arrived. The requirement was that the data never leave, and no measured rate can turn that into a guarantee. What kind of control can?

Fire the control before the data crosses the boundary§5.3

A required control cannot depend on the model behaving correctly, because probabilistic behaviour does not produce a guarantee. The pattern is to classify, then de-identify or tokenise before the call, then re-associate locally and audit the crossing.

The boundary is the edge of the system or network the data is permitted to stay in, such as the client's own network. The timing test is blunt: a control that acts after the request is assembled is too late, because the crossing has already happened.

Inference: in free-text narratives, finding the identifiers can itself rely on a detector that can miss, so the design keeps the re-identification map and the audit inside the boundary. The corpus point stands: the control acts before the call and does not depend on the main model complying.

Three things fail to turn a tendency into a guarantee: high measured accuracy, documented residual risk, and a larger model.

If regulated identifiers must never reach the inference endpoint, de-identify or tokenise in the pipeline before the call. A system-prompt instruction to ignore identifiers is rejected, since it is evaluated after the identifiers are in the request and depends on compliance. Nightly redaction of stored logs is rejected as the control, because it fixes the record and not the transmission.

The reverse case also exists. A strong preference, such as the tone wanted in redactions, is a tendency, and a prompt instruction is the fitting mechanism for it.

Ask which constraint binds: the payload or the environment§5.4

The corpus names what each regime forces into the design. GDPR covers the personal data of people in the EU. HIPAA covers protected health information in the US. FedRAMP covers cloud services used by US federal agencies. GDPR and HIPAA mostly constrain what is in the request, and FedRAMP mostly constrains where the request may be processed. This is the course corpus's reading of each regime and not legal advice.

Three further points from the corpus complete the picture. GDPR also requires being able to account for automated decisions that affect a person. HIPAA allows the processor to be brought inside the covered boundary under agreement, as an alternative to de-identifying. FedRAMP constrains deployment topology as well as the list of models, and it expects documented control implementation.

For EU customer records feeding a support assistant, minimise the fields to what the task needs, declare the purpose, make sure the endpoint region is permitted, and list every store for erasure. For clinical narratives drafted by an assistant, strip direct identifiers before the call, send the narrative, reattach locally, and log access.

For a federal agency whose preferred model is not in an authorised environment, the model is unavailable for that data: route through an authorised environment, or keep the data out. FedRAMP is a boundary and procurement fact and not a configuration setting.

When a GDPR erasure request arrives for a customer whose tickets seeded an evaluation dataset and a vector index, deletion must reach the logs, the dataset and the index, since derived data is still that person's data.

Retain with a purpose and a duration, and keep the audit record unalterable§5.5

Retention requires a stated purpose and a bounded duration. Storage is a decision that creates obligations, and "it might be useful later" is not a purpose. Every retained interaction is erasure scope, breach surface and discoverable material. A team that proposes keeping every request and response indefinitely for future evaluation work is told to define the purpose and the duration, and to de-identify what is kept.

The audit record holds what was sent, when, under what purpose, which model and prompt version, what returned and the outcome.

It must be immutable, because an audit trail that can be edited is not evidence. Inference: immutability and erasure meet by de-identifying what the record holds and by deleting under a stated policy, a point to settle with counsel. If auditability is met only by application logs that the operations team can edit, reject it.

An incident investigation that needs recent traffic gets a bounded window, for example 30 days, de-identified and purpose-coded. If a regulator may ask why a specific decision was made, retain the decision trace under its own policy. Explainability needs the trace, which is narrower and longer-lived than raw logs.

Go deeper: what indefinite retention adds up to

This is a worked example, not a corpus rule, and the volume is invented. At 50,000 requests a day, a 30-day window holds 1,500,000 interactions at any time. Kept indefinitely, the store reaches 18,250,000 after one year, and it keeps growing.

Each of those interactions is something an erasure request has to find, something a breach exposes, and something a discovery process can demand. A scheduled redaction job over that store treats the problem after the decision to store the field has been made, which is why the corpus calls it a repair and not a control.
