A year after a claims system went live, a new engineer asks why the routing step runs on the smaller model. The latency ceiling has just been raised from 3 seconds to 6, and the bigger model is tempting. The original team has moved on. The code shows which model is used, and it does not show why, or whether the reason still holds.

The reader who asks that question is the person documentation exists for. They are not asking what the system does, since the code says that. They are asking why it is like this and whether they may change it. What would the record have to contain to answer?

Record why, so the decision can be reopened§6.7

The corpus discriminator is whether a reader who was not present can tell a constraint that mattered from a preference. An architecture decision record has four parts: the decision, the alternatives considered, the trade-offs, and the constraints that drove the choice. For an AI system, also record the evaluation numbers the decision rested on and the condition that reopens it.

A record of the chosen design answers "what". Only the alternatives, the trade-offs and the driving constraints answer "why, and may I change it". When a model-tier choice is made under a latency ceiling, record the decision, the rejected options with their numbers, the trade-off and the ceiling. When the ceiling moves, the decision can be reopened on evidence. Constraints expire, and an unrecorded one makes the decision impossible to reopen.

If a year-old decision is questioned after a constraint changed, check the recorded constraint and re-evaluate against it, which is the moment the record exists for. A detailed component diagram and inventory is insufficient alone, since diagrams answer "what" and carry no reasoning. A recorded walkthrough is insufficient alone as well. It transfers knowledge once, and it goes stale on the first change.

Go deeper: why the numbers belong in the record

This is a worked example, not a corpus rule, and the figures are invented. The larger model scored 1.3 points higher on a 400-claim set, which is 5.2 claims. A successor who knows that can judge whether the gap is real, and a record that says only "the larger model was better" cannot be re-run.

The 2.1 seconds also has a price. At 40,000 requests a day it adds 84,000 seconds of waiting, about 23.3 hours a day. With the ceiling raised to 6 seconds the cost may now be affordable, and the record tells the successor which two numbers to measure again.

Give the receiving team what lets them change it safely§6.8

The corpus discriminator is whether the receiving team can build and change the system without reconstructing the architect's reasoning. Implementation guidance holds four things: interface contracts, the rationale behind configuration values, the failure modes with the response to each, and the tests that must pass before a change ships. It is distinct from the decision record, which explains why the design is as it is.

If another team will extend the pipeline, give them interface contracts, configuration rationale, failure modes and gating tests, so that they can change it safely without the original team. If a prompt or model change is proposed after handover, name the regression tests that must pass first in the guidance. That prevents a silent behavioural regression from shipping.

An onboarding overview document is a supplement and not a substitute, since it orients a reader and does not gate a change. Configuration values documented without their rationale are insufficient, because a value without its reason is changed by the next person who finds it inconvenient.

An internal team will take over extending a delivered Claude system.

  • CorrectProvide interface contracts, the rationale behind each configuration value, the known failure modes with the response to each, and the tests that must pass before any change ships.
  • WrongARCHITECTEDProvide a thorough narrative overview of how the system works end to end. It reads as complete documentation and gates nothing.
