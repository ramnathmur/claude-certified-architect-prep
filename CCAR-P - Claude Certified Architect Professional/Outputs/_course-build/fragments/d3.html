Domain 3 is the heaviest at 19%, about 12 of the 63 items, and it has more objectives than any other domain: eight. Domains 1 and 2 are about the system you design. Domain 3 is about everything that system has to touch and that you did not design.

Think of an identity provider configured years ago, a document store whose refresh job runs at 02:00, a downstream service that is slow on one request in a hundred, and a ticketing API that predates REST conventions.

The eight objectives look scattered. One question runs through all of them: what gets admitted into the model's context or reach, what does the admission cost, and what record do you keep of it? The table below is the course's reading of the domain, not the guide's wording.

ObjectiveThe question it answers
Capability bloatHow many capabilities enter the agent's decision space?
Authentication and authorizationWhose privileges does the agent exercise when it reaches out?
Accuracy and latencyWhat does each admitted thing cost in time?
Observability at scaleWhat record do you keep of what was admitted, and why?
RAG chunking and indexingWhat shape does admitted knowledge arrive in?
Retrieval strategyWhich knowledge is admitted for this query?
Connection protocolsThrough which door does anything get admitted at all?
Progressive discovery and monolithic contextDoes everything get admitted up front, or on demand?

Questions in this domain rarely ask what something is. They give you a production system that is misbehaving, or a design proposal with a cost or an SLA attached, and ask which change addresses the cause. The one official Domain 3 sample shows the pattern: the right answer removes two tools, and the wrong answers add logging, a confirmation prompt or a larger model.
